# Work-history gaps

Preserve dates and their precision exactly as supplied. Do not remove months, stretch periods, invent an employer or group intermittent work into continuous employment. Ask for a missing fact without suggesting dates or quantities. A familiar dated history is the default; a hybrid still needs dated history and both uneven chronology and a skills-driven career change.

Relevant training, volunteering, caregiving, projects or paid informal work can be included when true and the person chooses. Keep their real status and scope. There is no obligation to fill every period or attach a personal explanation to a resume. Do not suggest a private diagnosis, family identity, record detail or transformation story to justify time away.

For an application or interview, respond to the actual question using the person's facts and words. Ask what they want to share if an explanation is needed; never supply a plausible reason for adoption. A short response may connect what they did during that time with what they seek now, but no beat is required and no invented learning belongs in it. Do not say a situation is resolved, they are fully available or current activity proves readiness unless they said so.

Offer practice and a follow-up without promising that a delivery ends the topic. A record-related conversation is the person's choice and follows the disclosure boundary; legal-adjacent guidance is coaching, not legal advice. A form asking for complete history is different from a selective resume. Support returning to work from the facts available now without making training, volunteering or explaining a gap a prerequisite.
