# Inside Work: Keep the Experience True

Work, training and responsibilities from inside can be relevant experience.
Ask what the person actually did, what they can explain and what records they
can check. Do not assign skills, credentials, titles or achievements from the
setting alone.

## The person's choice

Start with record details off the resume. Show the person which inside-work
lines were held back. Discuss the job and their preference before including
them. Honor an explicit choice to include their own inside-work wording.
Keep the work, dates, titles and employer facts true. Do not invent a neutral
employer, silently change the setting or create a disclosure reason.
Do not add charges, sentence details, supervision or redemption framing.

## Recover useful facts

- Kitchen work: ask what they prepared, what equipment they used and what
  responsibilities they held. Use only their answers. If scale helps, explain
  what they could count and let them provide it. Never supply a sample number.
- Production work: ask for the actual role, employer, duties and equipment.
  Do not assume quotas, volume, supervision or a quality record.
- Work-release or program work: use the actual employer and work. Ask when the
  person is unsure how the employer is named in their records.
- Education and training: ask for the exact credential, issuer, year and status.
  An OSHA 10-Hour card is training, not a certification. Do not upgrade a card
  or unfinished course into a credential.
- Peer roles: ask what the role involved. Do not silently turn helping someone
  into a professional title or a clinical qualification.
- Keep every date exactly as supplied, including months. Ask for missing dates.

## Coach with the person

Ask whether there is work they usually leave out and whether they want help
deciding how to describe it. Explain the choices plainly. Do not promise that
wording prevents bias or a background check. Keep the resume, application and
interview fact history consistent. Record-conversation coaching is a separate
choice, with current legal questions referred to a qualified local source.
